What the benchmark is for

Static success rate cannot tell a lucky miss from a controlled stop. RoboSafeBench scores both the ending and the motion that got there.

01

One ladder, three simulators

The same L0–L3 disturbances run on LIBERO, SimplerEnv, and RoboTwin.

02

Disturbances that actually move

L1 suddenly occupies the workspace, L2 slides through it, L3 is a reaching hand. The policy must react while the task is still live.

03

Contact counts

Every episode records success, contact, peak force, and topple. Joint quadrants split Safe Success from contact-success and contact-free failure.

04

Process, not only endings

Behavior diagnostics cut the rollout at onset: did the policy react, stop, evade, then recover? Ten mutually exclusive classes replace a single “unsafe” bucket.

The question

Most VLA tables report success in a still scene.

RoboSafeBench asks what happens when the workspace is no longer still.

LIBERO-Safety evaluates physical safety and semantic refusal on the LIBERO stack. VLA-Arena measures capability frontiers with L0–L2 difficulty plus language/visual probes, and reports cumulative cost on Safety. RoboSafeBench evaluates the same timed, moving-contact protocol on three manipulation simulators, with process diagnostics; semantic refusal is out of scope.
